## Three hypotheses — different market states and exact intended signals

**1. LEVEL_REJECTION_M5 (priority 1):** A prior completed eligible M5 range high or low is fully known. The test candle later breaches that boundary yet closes BACK INSIDE. Rejected upper break -> SHORT; rejected lower -> LONG. Conditional future M5 Open only after T10 or T15 signal delivery; Stop beyond visible rejection extreme. Failure questions: did reversal finish before delayed entry; was structural Stop huge; can market plausibly travel 3 net-loss units in remaining session; do enough independent tests exist? OHLCV does not reveal stop orders or actual liquidity sweeps.

**2. LEVEL_BREAKOUT_RETEST_M5 (priority 2):** Use the same type of past-established level, but a later completed M5 CLOSES OUTSIDE it. This defines a provisional accepted break, not an entry. Wait for another later completed price retest of broken area and still later directional continuation/hold before delayed entry; structural Stop beyond retest. Unlike failed Session Momentum do not chase initial breakout. Failure: three confirmations plus T10/T15 exhaust the session or leave 3R infeasible. The price-action claim is not a statistically proven MOEX edge.

**3. SWING_PULLBACK_M5 (priority 3):** Establish local direction from exclusively prior completed persistent intraday swing structure rather than a single qualified prior breakout level. A separate later countertrend retrace followed by new confirmed continuation triggers a delayed M5 Open; Stop beyond corrective swing. Treat a level-breakout retest as candidate 2, NEVER also count it in candidate 3. Failure: ambiguous swing definition (must become deterministic before any counts), noisy mean-reversion conditions, large Stop, entry late after correction.

**No redundant strategy counts:** failed tests (close back inside) and accepted breaks (close outside) are mutually exclusive classifications; candidate 3 excludes candidate 2's qualified level retests. Count independent event IDs; a single instrument's simultaneous signals cannot all become concurrent trades. Portfolio 2–3/day ambition cannot simply sum three unadjusted setup counts.

## Research sources — motivation, not proven MOEX advantage

Carol Osler describes both reversals at prior-known FX support/resistance and acceleration once such levels are crossed based on actual interbank stop/take orders: [New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr125.html), [Journal of Finance 2003](https://doi.org/10.1111/1540-6261.00588). An M5 price break is **not** direct evidence of stop orders; these observations do not validate the precise false-break/retest rules. [15-year intraday US futures reversal study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378426604000949) highlights cost sensitivity. [Szakmary et al. 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S037842660900199X) studies trend following at MONTHLY horizons; it is NOT M5 pullback evidence. [2023 commodity futures reversal/semivariance](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2023.03.001) uses different data/signals and does not predict MOEX performance. No verified paper proves our PF, 3R, frequency or positive-month targets.

## Exact model 3R calculation — do not conflate two ratios

Let s = gross initial entry-to-stop distance, t = dated tick used as C1 for each entry and exit, d = gross planned entry-to-take distance. C1 total two ticks (no extra broker fees).

Original frozen reporting used a net-win / gross-stop-risk ratio: (d−2t)/s >=3, equivalently d>=3s+2t. **The literal entirely after-cost win / after-cost Stop loss ratio** is (d−2t)/(s+2t) >=3, or d>=3s+8t for an ideal stop. Example with Stop four ticks: gross target 14 ticks yields net win twelve, net loss six => **2:1**, not 3:1. A gross target of twenty ticks yields net eighteen against loss six => **3:1**. Actual adverse Stop gaps worsen the realized ratio. Preserve old frozen definitions and report BOTH on the new candidates; preregister the more conservative literal full-net 3R for new feasibility and future Baseline. This may further reduce entry frequency: report that result, do not soften it after observing counts.

A simplified fixed 3:1 net binary gamble requires a >25% win rate for positive expectancy, about 34.8% for PF 1.6, and 40% for PF 2.0; actual varying payoffs, early exits, costs and gaps prevent directly applying those thresholds. These are illustrations, not trade forecasts.
